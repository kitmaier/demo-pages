Circle Covering Puzzle

This is based on a puzzle from the YouTube channel 3Blue1Brown.

The puzzle is to determine whether 10 arbitrary points in the plane can always be covered by nonoverlapping unit discs.

If the number of points were small, such as up to 3 or 4 points, then it would always be easy to cover them with discs.

If the number of points were very large, then there would be simple ways of arranging them that could not be covered. For instance, a large grid of closely spaced points cannot be covered by nonoverlapping discs because when three discs get very close together, there is always a small curved wedge in between them that cannot be covered.

In fact, this can even be achieved with only two short lines of closely spaced points. Here the lines are long enough that they cannot be covered by a single disc, so in one of the lines there must be two consecutive points that are covered by two different discs. In order for these discs not to overlap, they must be arranged nearly side by side in a small range of positions. The second line of dots can be placed a short distance away such that the first two discs must have a gap into which at least one dot falls, which cannot be reached by a third disc. To be determined is the minimum number of dots in this arrangement that cannot be covered, it is estimated to be less than 100.
